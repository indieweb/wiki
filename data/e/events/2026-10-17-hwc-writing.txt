== Styles ==

* Moving between different styles in our writing.
* Do bloggers move between styles like musicians?
* Looking back lets us see a style at the time (different from writing to a style though).
* Twitter changing writing -- writing to 140 chars; having limited space while trying to get something cohesive out.
** For Thomas, longer writing got much longer at the same time as posting more to Twitter.
* We might also stick to a style.
* Tracy did a year-long/nine-months joint blog / email newsletter during the pandemic that was personal memoir in short format. https://sensememory.cascadiainspired.com
* Album framing: the blog as a body of work.
* Joe, ending introspective posts with "Onward."
** Knowing he had that destination of the final word impacted the writing process.
** Times were difficult when this was used.
* Joe's newsletter tone is slightly different to website: aims to be succinct, and give message for the week since the email goes out on a Monday.
* Tone and how it lands is something to think about while you write (does it fit what you want to communicate?).
* How something resonates can differ between people; something might be felt deeply by one person while you didn't expect that during the writing process.
** Everyone has their own context, and multiple contexts, and so it's hard to anticipate how something will be received.
* Poetry has its own performance context
* You can imbue something into prosaic text when it is read in a certain way.
* Recently watched this vid on the history of Scary Stories to Tell in the Dark - it mentions the author writing and in the writing process reading and rereading the work aloud to "get it right"

== Cadence and speaking styles ==

* James' writing is closer to how he thinks (poetically, optimistically) than how he talks with others (which is a bit more playful and less poetic, although poetic elements do come through in questions)
* How you write depends on who you are writing for
* News anchors have their own cadence; politicians, too.
* Speech delivery is a skill.
* Speech writing is a skill, too; speeches need to be written with the context of who is going to say it. How people pronounce words, their cadence, accents, etc. all come into speech writing. You want to keep a good rhythm in the speech that the speaker can naturally bring in.
** Listening to people to understand how they speak is important.
** The person who reads the speech should familiarise themselves with the text.
* Book recommendations: https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/13155290-several-short-sentences-about-writing
* https://archive.org/details/severalshortsent00klin
