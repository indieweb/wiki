=== Q: How to stop fiddling with site? ===
* Learning to leaving the site alone - constantly building new features, improving it
** Building a healthier relationship so that you're not tinkering all the time
** Tinkering is so fun! (which maybe is what makes it harder not to do as much technical stuff)
** Or maybe it's okay to have little things to work on for your website to give you momentum
*** Naty relates to this a lot as she is constantly tinkering/learning and gets distracted from writing
* When ideas come in put them in a Google Keep (or note app) to be visited later
** Don't try to add it immediately
* You can make side websites / single pages if you want that don't have to be constantly maintained
* You could write about your ideas rather than trying to build everything
** There is always the risk of burnout if you do too much at once
* Obsidian brings the content out of the IDE, so there's less chance for tinkering for Quartz users
** You add drafts to Obsidian, then push directly to live site without looking at the backend of Quartz
* Having "natural stops" in our work?
* Having a separation of task types can be helpful
* Making it only possible to do something on your site on your computer (i.e publishing)
* Tinkering is like a dopamine hit because it's the reward of being able to build something after learning something new for example
* WinnerWind wrote a SSG from scratch, but built it like a house of cards, so it limits what he can do; adding a new feature will probably break it, so that stops him from changing too much
* Find something better to do, that's not personal websites!
* Writing thoughts down so you can get them out of your head and move on to new thoughts

=== Writing is fun, editing can feel like work ===
* Nadja: Having lots of thoughts at a fast rate means that you might end up with incomplete sentences or things that don't flow as well
** You can start with one topic and end up with ten different posts
** Editing is necessary to keep things on topic
* Hemingway: "Write drunk, edit sober." (the editing process is never fun, but it is necessary)
* JL: Doing research before writing a blog post, and doing a stream of consciousness, then chipping away at the text
** Can take 1-2 months to write a full article
* Giving people grace when they write on their websites
** Not everyone is a professional writer
** It's okay to make typos, etc!
** James issued https://jamesg.blog/2026/10/27/typo-correction recently because of a typo (which, now, he knows four people saw!)
* Syncing Obsidian with computer and phone is much appreciated by Morgan
** Recently posted a piece with an admonition, warning it was put up quickly/might have typos; luckily someone working at Google proofread his post and caught his typo
* Having a proofreader can help (you can always ask a friend you know for help!)
* When you read something you wrote immediately after, you can be too close to the text and it is harder to spot problems
** It can take a while to find a good proofreader
