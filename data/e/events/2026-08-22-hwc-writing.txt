== Notes ==
* Welcome to HWC Writing Edition
* For the next 90 minutes we'll have time to chat about all things writing on the web.
* Writing books is hard
** Definitely! even making a small zine can be hard. Ownership is tough and gratifying
** Writing books is a process
* Writing helps us build mental models

* How do you challenge yourself as a writer
** Writing something that might have effects / have the potential to have an impact (i.e. change someone's mind on a topic, something that people can use).
** Looking out for what other people misunderstand in general can help you improve your writing.
*** Having a good editor can help you, especially for larger projects. Editors can help you stay focused, and give you feedback that you can think through as you keep writing. Feedback from readers can help too.
** Being surrounded by people who are sharing new ideas.
** Reading different resources, looking out for different mediums.
** Trying poetry.
* It's sometimes hard to know how much to talk about your work.
** You can syndicate what you write to other places, or announce to other places, like LinkedIn for professional pieces, etc.
** For personal writing you might not syndicate because it is more personal / random.
** You might have a specific place / website where you focus on subjects in more depth (i.e for professional writing).
** When you publish via web feeds, you may be conscious how your post will be announced to people who follow you.
* Point of tension: when I feel like what I have written isn't important enough to write down.

* Journaling: using nice pen, dating pieces of work. You can carry a notebook that's for everyday use. 
** "Oh, it's a Moleskine. I have to put my big thoughts in there." -- how fancy tools can limit us.
* Some things in Notes apps gets lost, and journals too.
* Ideas weather away in Notes without being consulted.
* Looking back on notes can give you inspiration, or trigger memories.
* Obsidian is a popular note-taking app. Having sync between desktop and mobile is good; it can give you a bit of a boost knowing your notes are automatically where you need them.
* Paper journals are not searchable technologically.
* Always carrying a notebook (i.e. in your backpack).

* Apple Notes: changing an entry will move it back to the top of your list.
** Obsidian has a randomise (?) feature, which can be useful, but not as "nutritious" as working methodically through your notes. You may need to add it to your menu palette.

* Dream journaling
** If you write your dreams down, you can have a greater recollection of them.
* Writing down phrases and snippets as a creative exercise -- thoughts that come to mind.
* Writing can take multiple drafts. Striking the right tone can be difficult.
* Seeing your own word play can keep you engaged and entertained.
* Looking through your old work can be hard, especially at first.

* Writing with editors can help you write things more simply, reducing sentence length.
* Self-editing can be tricky. Editing is a skill.
* Knowing what tone you want to use (i.e. keeping a light tone, adding some snark, being serious, etc.).
* If you are writing to explain, you need to keep things simple so it is easy for people to follow your logic and works.

* Native languages are our instruments: we can use it to communicate, write a blog post, writing something of a journalistic quality. We might use our language as source material to create art, for poetry, writing a novel.
* Reading a translation when you know both languages can make you realise there's a missing connection; something has been lost in translation; a spirit, connections to different elements of culture.
* To understand a single word of a foreign language, you have to understand the whole culture. Even a word like "rabbit" can have different meanings (pretty, source of food).
* Language is a treasure.

** As a writer, being aware of this can make it harder to find the momentum.
** "Does the reader have this background?" is a good question to ask as you write.
