== IndieAuth ==

* Stephan was thinking about adding IndieLogin to his website
* People who log in via IndieAuth bring their own identity provider, which could be GitHub, Bluesky, or an email, or something else.
* https://indielogin.com/setup
* carrvo is using a self-hosted IndieAuth identity provider
** The Mindie project was named as a short version of "Minimal indieauth"
* [[IndieAuth]] has more information about IndieAuth too
* If you register via IndieAuth, can you get an email?
** You can, via the "email" scope: https://indieauth.spec.indieweb.org/20220212/#example-13
** An application could ask if an email doesn't exist
** Many applications let you pick and choose your scopes, rather than accept all of them as is common with other tools
** [[consent_screen]]
*** IndieKit example: [[File:indiekit-consent-screen-with-client-info.png]]

** https://artemis.jamesg.blog (invite code "coffee")
** see more places to test here: [[IndieAuth]]_clients
* carrvo is interested in making a web chat powered by Webmentions

* Stephan has something to demo
** community hub and portal for "internet art" https://asatte.io/
** goal is to connect internet artists, is community-driven
* https://www.are.na/
** [[Are.na]]
** Often bookmarks of other properties
** Used by artists to curate things, but also to upload documents like zines, PDFs, photos, etc.
** Unlike Pinterest, Arena doesn't force you to sign up / present lots of paywalls
** When you sign up, Arena doesn't tell you to follow lots of people; Arena isn't designed to force things on you.
*** A quiet onboarding for a quiet tool.

* Joe updated https://joecrawford.com/
*** has evolved over the years, current iteration is a landing page
*** designed in procreate > photoshop > AVIF
*** has subtle animations
** Also updated https://artlung.com/robots/, now has an image grid view and navigation by year (par of [[Weird Web October]])
* Default controls are different on video elements across browsers
** https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn_web_development/Core/Accessibility/Multimedia
** Removing default controls means you need to account for accessibility
** Using a JS library is an option, but you still need to consider accessibility
* Customisable select in CSS; is likely more accessible than building your own
** https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn_web_development/Extensions/Forms/Customizable_select
* [[Front_End_Study_Hall]] is every two weeks or so
* Implementing emails and passwords
* how to implement tiered access to our websites without storing emails and passwords
** lots of libraries available in many languages for dealing with the basics of password security and account login
** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_minimization
** If you use IndieAuth for signin, you don't need to keep track of passwords at all!
** It's a good idea to reduce the amount of personal data that you store, and therefore are at risk of exposing.
** You can use signin links that expire so you don't have to store passwords
** If you have more time, create a dashboard on your service that has signin history (but this is location data that you have to consider).
** If you have even more time, add the ability to invalidate signin tokens.
** holding on to other people's personal data is not a responsibility to be taken lightly
** rel-me-auth and websignin are worth a look
** [[websignin]]
** If you are hashing and salting passwords, make sure you use a standard library like bcrypt, etc.
** If you use OAuth, you outsource auth to someone else which might be a good idea but also makes you depend on that service
** Risk minimisation is important when designing your authentication systems

== Demo guidance ==

* Focus on one thing you want to share
* Share something you think will be interesting to the group (ideally something others can use)
* Keep your demo under 2 mins long
* Focus more on what you made (rather than the tech details); people interested in the tech side can always ask after the demo.
