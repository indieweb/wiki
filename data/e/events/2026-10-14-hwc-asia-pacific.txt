==== Learning Journey ====
* Once you are in the weeds and spend time learning, things start to make sense
* Taking breaks is important when you're learning technical skills (or anything for that matter!)
* There are lots of walled gardens on the web
** We forget that there is more on the internet to read and see
* Social apps don't teach the younger generation HTML or how a site is displayed (CSS); they abstract all the foundations and limit the ability for self expression (structurally and stylistically speaking)
* Learning the basis of HTML isn't tricky; you can get a site working in a browser quickly everyone can do it
* It can be easy to lose the passion to learn something new, but it pays off!
* Automation and AI reliance is diverting our curiousity to learn how to do things from the foundational level
** Excitement when we learn something is diminishing, the art of perseverance is getting lost
* MySpace was customizable, everyone has unique profiles, which sparks curiousity 
** "Wow, how did they do that?"
** The feeling on FB from the early days of knowing you're not allowed to make many/any changes to the style; everyone's profile looks the same
*** Today we're so used to that paradigm
** Back then it wasn't "are you technical or not?", it was the curiosity that led you to experimentation
** The average person has been so far removed from the technical details
* The word "Markdown" has slightly broader use than exclusively among technical professionals
** AI may be changing things; people might not make a website themselves
*** But it does open the mental gates that it is possible for things to be different
* Having a personal site is a learning experience
** For example, when you fix your XML you might accidentally cause people to see old posts again
*** Jeremy gotten 70 new items from Naty because she was fixing her XML (Sorry about that! XML feeds are scary to get wrong —Naty)

==== XML / Feeds ====
* Q: How to follow people's new posts/content?
** Most people use feeds!
** There are feed readers that you can use to follow feeds
** Most sites have some kind of feed (or topic/section specific ones) that you can follow *** And, if not, maybe a newsletter to which you can subscribe by email
** Naty was fixing her feed earlier. Some things you change in a feed can make some readers see posts as new posts.
* Q:What feed readers do people use?
** Feeder https://f-droid.org/en/packages/com.nononsenseapps.feeder/
** FeedFlow https://f-droid.org/en/packages/com.prof18.feedflow are two Naty tried on Android
** NetNewsWire is hard to beat on iOS/Mac https://netnewswire.com/
** [[feed_reader]] has a list of feed readers

* https://artemis.jamesg.blog (invite code is "coffee")

** Mastodon also offers email subscription, or at least has capability to do that
** Mastodon RSS doesn't offer all the information though (i.e. content warnings)

*** May be important if the page has interactive elements, or a custom design, or something else that will be lost in the text-focused reader experience

==== Data Portability ====
* Transportability is important when deciding backend / generator / CMS
* Q: How easily can one move from one platform generator to another?
* HTML comes into play because it will probably last for a long time just like .txt will last
* For writing / text-based sites, something simple is important to focus on the content: word choice, construction, spelling
* The backend logic / code can be complex, but content should be easily transportable (ensure Markdown stays human-readable), source of truth should be clean
** E.g. by not including Liquid or Nunjucks
* Considering abstraction is part of the design of a static site (i.e. keep complex logic in custom templates)
* Naty and Jeremy both use front matter in Markdown
** Naty uses a few Hugo shortcodes to add extra features into markdown; tries to limit use
*** Regex/search and replace should change things if needed to migrate
** At beginning, Naty didn't know as much about portability, so shortcodes was used extensively; but now she knows to KISS (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KISS_principle)
* James encountered this in the past, where he wrote some code for a game for filling in song lyrics
** But when he changed global site styles, that page broke
*** Now tries to sandbox interactive or "special" pages
** Example: https://jamesg.blog/taylor-swift-quiz
*** The styles are not expected; the borders are not correct in dark mode
* Q: Does anyone else break things on their website?
** —yes :D —yes too!

==== Site Navigation / Menus ====
* Menu design is something to consider when you are working on a website
* Single pages not in the menu
* Running out of space in the menubar, not sure where to place possible new slash pages e.g. /now or /coffee
** Using checkbox hack dropdown for menu; apparently it is not too good for accessibility
*** Naty uses plain text links now: "Explore" link skips to a specific part of the page as an anchor (#) link
* Menus can be deceptively tricky, it's not really just a list of links
** Need to choose the most important ones
** Like a chef's menu; it is curated and can change depending on what's important to the author
* Explore link can point to a categories page
* Menus as a way to guide your audience, and as a way to express what is most important to you
* Menus change as the webmaster changes!

==== Perfectionism ====
* Q: How do people approach treading the same ground again?
** Feel need to get perfection for definitive post on some topic
** Writing it in weeknote feels misplaced?
** How to categorize more casual vs professional pieces?
* Standards on oneself is often higher than others
** Sitting with the feeling of anxiety but push on helps reset the mindset
* We're often being harder on ourselves than anyone else is going to be
** You can always go back and edit your blog posts; don't stress on it too much
* Doing something over and over again trains your mind to not be so hard on yourself
* James also felt that even after multiple edits of one of his new posts, he's unsureif it should be published
** But still happy it is finally on his site!
* By letting yourself write the same topic multiple times, you might discover new or interesting content
** There can still be something there even if it isn't obvious at first
* At some point in history there was no dictionary; more words were invented, the dictionary was made more comprehensive with new volumes
** Chasing definitive posts is the aim, but it is not realistic; we can always revisit
** Always opportunities to tread, reframe
** Biggest pressure: encountering the feeling that something has to be perfect now.
** https://jamesg.blog/romantics-vi
