=== Tools and trends of personal sites ===
* James feels this year has been the most active year since he joined the IndieWeb (~5-6 years ago).
** There are many services popping up that are available for people to discover creators on the small web
** The trend of having personal websites is perhaps on the rise?
* Personal sites is a place for sharing human stories
* The ability to put a "personal touch" or "look of an indie site"
* Building stuff manually can be hard at times but it puts you in the mode of the basics which can be a good mindset to have — focusing on a good foundation like having a good semantic HTML structure
** https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/curriculum/core/semantic-html/
** Learning the basics before you enter something like WordPress, etc. can do wonders
* Coding can sometimes distract you from writing when you are just setting up a website, but you can end up learning a lot about how the web works and becoming a semi web-dev (inadvertently)
** Main building blocks are HTML and CSS for styling
* Websites as a place to explore personal style
* James built a site with React before, and then decided to just go to plain HTML as there is no frameworks to rely on
** HTML is long lasting, parts of React will break probably after some years 
* Sites made a long time ago will still work if they are written in HTML! PHP has some longevity too
* WordPress is its own kind of PHP with its own specific functions
* WP security vulnerabilities are frequent and getting more serious perhaps in the age of AI
** https://www.wordfence.com/blog/2026/09/5-million-wordpress-sites-affected-by-sql-injection-vulnerability-in-all-in-one-wp-migration-and-backup-wordpress-plugin/
* Keeping it simple, just make a HTML with notepad!
* Nadja shares that ~95% of their content has been lost, e.g. [[site-deaths]]
** "Minimalism is a gift to building enduring content"
** "Shiny things don't last, especially those built with new tooling or frameworks"
** Nadja has wondered why web developers' sites are always very simple looking. Because masters of the craft realizes what lasts
* Gen Z are going back to retro / analogue concepts and practices (e.g. physical media, radio)
** "Perfectionism is not the way forward"
*** James was listening to the radio this morning haha
** "Simple is better; we can show our humanity on the web"
* AI usually cannot create sites that are "lightweight", Naty came across this page last week:
** https://async.cat/taste
* There are groups that promote small / lightweight sites
** https://1kb.club/ and https://512kb.club/
* Tame shares https://tavernary.org/ this is the shittily AI designed website by Claude

=== Finding community ===
* "Who will see my site? I'll be just one page in a million on the internet." 
** Communities like the IndieWeb + 32 Bit Cafe are ways to meet new friends, find community, niche hobbies, etc
** People will see you eventually when you start writing things!
** Not everyone will comment; some sites don't have comments either
** If you provide an email or contact form, people do reach out if they see something that they relate to
** Don't feel discouraged at the beginning. There isn't really immediate feedback on the IndieWeb but it will eventually come, the concept of a "slower web"
** There are people who blog about everything, check out [[blogroll]] for inspiration
* When we visit personal sites, we want to hear the human voice, the story and interests of the creator
** There is usually a lot of knowledge behind every person's site and the experiences they are sharing
** You get to see all these unique websites that you wouldn't see on social media. You can even change the colour / design of the feed.
** Everyone has their own unique interests.
* Naty likes tech, photography, fountain pens, and more. Still working on putting things on her website.
** Built a photo section on her site to import coffee posts from Instagram
*** Tried to "mimic Instagram", supports multiple images per post
** Motivation for building this was so that she could import her IG backlog and use Pagefind (static site search tool) to search for the content written in her IG posts, like her coffee notes
*** https://pagefind.app/
*** Wished she had written in plain Markdown first, then published to Instagram, so everything would at least be searchable
*** Related: [[POSSE]]
*** If you aren't already doing POSSE, now better time than now to do [[PESOS]] to pull your siloed content into your site
* Export formats can be hard to deal with
* It's important to create somewhere you can have full access / ownership to your content without being locked in
** Then you can share it to other places if you want to
** And perhaps share links to your site so you can get people still stuck in silos / walled gardens can get interested in the IndieWeb!
* The IndieWeb as a place for genuine connections. It's nice to see people putting a personal touch on things
** https://notes.jeddacp.com/the-beauty-of-the-everyday/

=== Social media's damages ===
* Deschooling concept — a deschooling of social media needs to happened; getting out of the mindsets of social media. Meta has been involved in many lawsuits:
** https://www.npr.org/2026/08/25/nx-s1-5944266/adam-mosseri-instagram-meta-child-safety-trial
* How social media relates to perfectionism — the idea that things need to be a certain way
** But there is a satisfaction in doing something for yourself: making things for the joy of it
* Hard to get rid of the conditioning of social media, the doom scrolling habits
** Realising how much the way we see the world has been influenced by social media
** Many of us didn't realise that this is what social media would do, both at an individual level and a community level
** "Can't you just enjoy it? Do you have to take a photo of it?"
* The Indieweb is incredible because it brings us back to the roots of doing something because it feels good
* There are people right now who are growing up with tech at a young age, even as young as a few years old
* We hope there is a way out from the current paradigm of likes, etc
* We can just do things for ourself every once in a while and that's okay!
* Substack, lots of feedback distraction
* Own site can remove the noise by just expressing oneself without needing "approval" by others
** Freedom of expression of any type of media on your own site: imagery, audio, words and more
** You can remove the distraction of likes, shares, algorithms, etc.
** We can experiment!

=== Q: Who's the audience? ===
* Do people in the IndieWeb also share what they make with people who aren't in the IndieWeb?
* James not sure when to share his website when chatting with friends
** Don't want to sound pushy or promoting/broacasting
* Jo: "I can show you this", then the person will be "Oh you have a website!" which is a easy way to share your site
** Some friends check occasionally and that's nice to know, but for close friends that's not necessary since they are up to date with your activity anyway
* Naty: having a separate website for personal writing would be ideal to have a space to share with friends that don't like tech talk / jargon (terms like NodeJS, Hugo or even HTML/CSS put some people off)
* Having a website doesn't/shouldn't mean that you are into technology
* The paralysis of not knowing where to begin
* Nadja: inspired to stop planning and start doing, is using Quartz too
* Finding people who knows what you're writing about and not alienating them
** Would it be helpful to use tags / categories?
** Categories as a way to help people find what they like / want to see
** James' example: https://jamesg.blog/categories
** fLaMEd's example: https://flamedfury.com/categories/
** Naty uses tags more consistently than "series": https://burgeonlab.com/tags
** You could have separate feeds for each category
* It's cool to share things so that anyone can come across them and learn from what we have learned!
** For example, Jo shared the dither image post with friends, and it resonated with people
** https://dead.garden/blog/how-my-images-are-dithered.html
** Visuals can help a lot to catch people's attention
* It's okay if friends aren't as interested in our website
** We should ask friends if we can mention them in a blog post before we publish
* Perhaps the audience of the site is ourselves, but still we want to make it easy for people to navigate / find things that are interesting to them
* Difference between someone who is familiar with site structures (i.e. tags) vs. someone who isn't as familiar
** This could make navigation easier or trickier
** Making a site accessible and easy to understand is something Naty (and I'm sure many other) strives to do
* An idea from Rajiv: Add a switch to toggle between seeing techy vs non-techy content, like a light/dark switch!
* Having a signal to point people to the right area is generally appreciated
** James is still unsure where to put the link to the category link, currently listed as "Explore" in his sidebar
** It helps people find things, but still need to think more
** Another idea is to use a filter to show/hide what the reader wants to see
** https://playground.jamesg.blog/ has filters that use CSS :has selectors and HTML checkboxes, and uses data- attributes to assign a category to each.
** https://muan.co/notes has filters too for notes
* Links to "next article" at bottom of page might help someone explore
** And having recent or related posts can also do that
* Having a site that represents you as a whole and having different categories/buckets can be hard to figure out in one's mind
* The feeling like we have to give up on one thing to go to the next. But we can do two or more things at once!
* Naty: "I want the site to encompass all the facets of me!"
* Social media makes us feel like we need to be "specialized", but personal sites can be more broad
** James learnt new things from reading stories from other bloggers
