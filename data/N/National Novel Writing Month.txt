'''<dfn>National Novel Writing Month</dfn>''' (AKA and hashtag '''NaNoWriMo''') was a site (<nowiki>nanowrimo(.)org</nowiki>) and challenge to write a 50,000-word novel (or other extended work) in the month of [[November]] that shut down in 2025.

== Shutdown ==
{{section-stub}}
The NaNoWriMo organization shut down in 2025 for a number of reasons:
* https://www.theguardian.com/books/2025/apr/02/creative-writing-nanowrimo-to-close-after-20-years
* 2025-10-26 [https://blakewatson.com/journal/nanowrimo-is-dead-long-live-nanowrimo/ NaNoWriMo is dead, long live NaNoWriMo!]
* 2025-04-02 [https://www.theguardian.com/books/2025/apr/02/creative-writing-nanowrimo-to-close-after-20-years Scandal-hit creative writing website NaNoWriMo to close after 20 years]

Despite the organization shut down, a few individuals continue to use the <kbd>#NaNoWriMo</kbd> hashtag to post about their writing projects during the month of November:
* https://indieweb.social/tags/NaNoWriMo
* https://twitter.com/hashtag/NaNoWriMo?f=live

== Criticism ==
In 2024 the NaNoWriMo organization [https://www.404media.co/nanowrimo-ai-policy-classist-ableist/ promoted gen AI tools], which caused controversy. 

== IndieWeb Examples ==
* {{chrisaldrich}} has [https://boffosocko.com/tag/nanowrimo/ posted on his personal site] about participating in NaNoWriMo in the past. It definitely helped to keep him accountable about portions of the process.
* {{tracydurnell}} posted daily [https://tracydurnell.com/tag/nanowrimo/ status updates] on her website instead of using the NaNoWriMo word count tracker in 2021

== Variants ==
Other versions included things like [https://www.phd2published.com/acwri-2/acbowrimo/about/ #AcWriMo] which involve academic/research writing.
